# Track: the best puzzles (research date 2026-11-05)

## 1. Key findings

FACTS (sourced; [n] = Sources list at bottom)
- NYT Games, 2024 plays (reported June 2025): 11.1B total; Wordle 5.3B, Connections 3.3B, Strands 1.3B. The Connections figure rests on one secondary mirror of a Verge report [1][2]. Daily-active users of the Games app rose from 886k (Oct 2022) to 2.6M+ (2023) [1]. NYT does not publish per-game daily players; blog figures such as "4.5M Wordle daily players" are unverified [2].
- NYT launch timeline: Connections beta Jun 2023; Strands Mar 2024; Pips official Aug 18 2025; Crossplay US Jan 2026; several experiments died fast (Digits 2023, Zorse 2025, Capture Feb 2026) [1]. Takeaway: even NYT kills most new formats.
- Wordle (Wardle, GDC Mar 2022): one puzzle a day, same for everyone, was a "mistake" that created the social aspect; emoji share grid "a huge part" of virality; 6 guesses; ~6 months playtesting; word list pruned from 13,000+; no ads/monetization by choice [3]. Wardle: "you can play it in three minutes, and that is all you get" (BBC, via [4]).
- Connections: three difficulty levers are word familiarity, ambiguity of categorization, and variety of wordplay; "more overlap makes a harder puzzle"; purple groups use rhymes/homophones/anagrams/fill-in-blank; process is creator -> editor -> tester panel. An LLM pipeline (GPT-4 creator, LLM editor, LLM ranker) produced puzzles solved 58.5% vs 69.1% for NYT (not significant, n=78 responses); intentional-overlap AI puzzles were rated harder than NYT in 60% of responses [5].
- Spelling Bee (Ezersky): starts from the pangram ("linchpin"); uses a database of candidates but says a human editor is needed to pick enjoyable ones; panel flags unfamiliar words; wants "smile-inducing aha moments"; avoids crossword-ese words [6].
- Puzzmo (Gage/Schlesinger; Hearst-owned): games are "sandboxes", built to be fun for ~10 hours total spread over a year, anti focus-group, "interestingness" = number of connections an idea has in your brain; many puzzles (Really Bad Chess, TypeShift) deliberately lack a single unique solution [7]. Gage: games should be algorithmic where possible, editors added if a hit [8 AMA via search; secondary]. Daily limits on purpose (Pile-Up Poker: 5 hands/day, he would prefer 3); one Spelunky round per day for 6 months made him better; wants players to share discoveries rather than gossip/outrage, citing Wordle [9]. Launch: ~100k people made 1M+ attempts to get in via invite keys [10].
- LinkedIn Games: Queens, Crossclimb, Pinpoint launched May 2024; Tango and Zip later [11]. 830,000 newsletter subscribers; LinkedIn says 84% of players return next day (Wired, May 1 2025) [12]. Every board is human-made (none AI/computer-generated); logic puzzle creator Thomas Snyder helps craft Queens/Zip; Snyder's framing: "trail of breadcrumbs" to the cabin [12]. Active-player counts not found.
- Nikoli school: exactly one solution reachable by logical deduction; no outside knowledge; symbol-based; easiest instances need only simple insights, hardest may need trial and error; build from a legal solution backwards, add clues only as needed, guard against alternate solutions [13]. Nikoli staff handcraft puzzles by policy [14, secondary].
- Generation reality check: a hobbyist Queens generator grows regions with backtracking to keep uniqueness; HN commenters note "put queens down first, then draw regions" is easy but uniqueness needs a solver-in-the-loop [15].
- Wordle-likes: Worldle was the most-searched spin-off in one study (37.2M avg searches, inflated by misspellings) [16]. Heardle was bought by Spotify July 2022 and shut May 5 2023; visits had fallen to 41M before the deal [17]. Bandle (John Levy) and Wikitrivia (Tom J. Watson, popular Jan 2022; Timeline-style ordering) are confirmed; Costcodle = guess a Costco product price in 6 tries; Timeguessr = guess place and year from a photo [18]. Reliable player counts for these: not found.
- Lichess: puzzles generated by mining 300M games and re-analyzing with Stockfish; each attempt treated as a rated game for puzzle difficulty; 3M puzzles attempted per day (older figure); 5.4M puzzles in the dataset (Oct 2025) [19].
- Mystery Hunt 2026: ~280 teams, 5,000+ participants; metas combine answers of many puzzles; constructors say a great "aha" is the hardest part to find [20].

INFERENCE (mine)
- The winners share one shape: tiny rules, a verifiable answer, a 2-10 minute session, a sharable result, and a fixed daily cadence. Way already has the cadence; it is weak on "tiny rule + visual feel + shareable result."
- Hand-making is a quality moat at NYT/LinkedIn, but Way has one owner. Generate-then-verify with a solver is the only way to volume; humans approve a short list.

## 2. Detail: what the loved games have in common

| Game | Core loop | Why it works (sourced where possible) | Machine-verifiable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wordle | 6 guesses, color feedback | Tiny rules, 3 minutes, same word for all, emoji share [3] | Yes (word list) |
| Connections | Group 16 words into 4 | Red herrings + overlap, 4 difficulty tiers [5] | Weak: categories are judgment-based; LLM drafts need human check |
| Strands | Theme word search | Hint + spangram; human editorial | Partly |
| Spelling Bee | Words from 7 letters | Progress ranks, pangram aha [6] | Yes (word list), but list is subjective |
| Mini / Midi crossword | Small crossword | Speed, leaderboard [1] | Yes, needs clues (human/AI) |
| Letter Boxed, Tiles, Pips | Constraint/logic, visual | Pips: domino regions, 3 difficulties; hand made (Drexel mag, secondary) | Pips yes (solver) |
| Queens (LinkedIn) | Place queens, 1 per row/col/region, no touching | Pure deduction, one solution, 3-8 min [11][15] | Yes |
| Tango | 0/1 grid with = and x clues | Binary-puzzle deduction | Yes |
| Zip | Draw a path through numbered cells covering the grid | Hamiltonian path, visual | Yes |
| Pinpoint | Guess category from clue words | Associative aha; judgment-based | Weak |
| Crossclimb | Word ladder + clues | Ladder constraint | Yes (word list) |
| Puzzmo set | 6 mini games, ~40 min | Variety, sandbox, anti-grind [7][10] | Mixed |

Design principles repeated across designers:
1. One daily, same for all, played in minutes, with a deliberate stop (Wardle, Gage) [3][9].
2. Rules learnable in one sentence; no outside knowledge (Nikoli) [13].
3. One solution by deduction, with a gentle ramp: easiest = simple insight [13].
4. A single "aha" is the product [6][20].
5. Result is tiny and sharable (emoji grid) [3].
6. Perceived fairness: failure should feel like the player's miss, not a trick (Gage on fairness, partial) [9].

Wordle-like breakouts: the pattern is "Wordle's shell + a different knowledge domain" (geography, film, music, ordering). Most lack lasting audience; the "communal phase" faded and Heardle died inside a corporate parent [17]. Lesson: domain-trivia games need lookup/knowledge, which conflicts with Way's "no lookup" rule. Skip.

Classic families (fit with Way)
- Nikoli grids: Sudoku, Kakuro, Nonogram, Slitherlink, Masyu, Hashi, Akari, Kakurasu. Strong, unique-solution, solver-verifiable, but each needs an interactive grid.
- Simon Tatham's collection: 39 puzzles with random generation [21]; a good source of "which mechanics are phone-friendly" and some open-source code to study (check license before reuse; not verified here).
- Logic grid (Einstein/zebra) puzzles: text clues, unique solution, solver-verifiable, can be answered with a short text ("who owns the fish"); medium UI.
- Knights and Knaves (Smullyan 1978) [22]: tiny text puzzles, brute-force verifiable by enumerating truth assignments; uniqueness is easy to check by script. Answer is a short string. Very good for Way's Logic slot.
- Cryptic crosswords: great aha, but clue quality and "fair surface" need human craft; answer text is checkable, soundness is not. Hard to produce at volume.
- Rebuses/word-picture, lateral thinking: aha-rich but answer ambiguity high; lookup-free but not machine-verifiable. Use sparingly with hand-written accepted-answer lists.
- Martin Gardner classics / river crossings / weighings / Monty-Hall style: short text answer, verifiable by search; excellent for Logic/Math.
- Chess tactics (Lichess model) [19]: solver-verifiable, but needs a board UI and chess knowledge; owner already has history with chess, and it was a failed long-term replacement. Fine as an optional weekly.
- Meta puzzles / Mystery Hunt [20]: multi-step; use in the "weekly deep" slot as a mini-meta (5 small answers produce 1 final word). High aha, high craft cost.
- Escape-room style: interactive state; no machine-verifiable core; skip.

## 3. Implications for Way (ranked)

Criteria: 2-10 min, no lookup, unique machine-checked answer, phone one-handed + keyboard, producible at volume, quick "I got it."

1. **Knights-and-knaves / truth-teller text puzzles (Logic slot).** What: 3-5 characters, statements, answer = who is what (e.g. "KNKK"). Why: tiny rule, Smullyan lineage [22], uniqueness and answer verified by brute-force script, AI can draft at volume. Fit: perfect; verifier gives zero wrong keys. Effort S. Needs no server; a tiny text/toggle answer UI.
2. **Logic-grid (zebra) puzzles, 3x3 to 4x4, answer as one short string.** Why: unique solution deduction (Nikoli principle [13]), solver-checkable, 5-10 min. Effort M (a small elimination-grid helper is optional; the answer can be typed). Static.
3. **Queens-style region puzzles (grid UI).** Why: LinkedIn's flagship [11][12], one-sentence rules, unique solution by deduction, generator + solver is well understood (regions with backtracking [15]), taps work on a phone. Fit: strong; no lookup; not programmer-only. Effort M-L (grid UI, tap cycle states, generator). Static; can pre-generate JSON offline and verify uniqueness with the script.
4. **Tango/binary and Zip/path puzzles (grid UI), as variants of the same grid component.** Why: same audience as Queens [11], uniqueness checkable. Effort M after #3 exists (reuse the component). Static.
5. **Word-ladder / letter-set Words hook with a solver (Spelling Bee- or Wordle-like).** Why: Wordle/Bee proven [3][6]; keeps Words slot as the diff-1 hook. Verifiable with a word list (watch list subjectivity; use one fixed open list and publish it). Effort S-M. Static; keyboard friendly. A Wordle clone itself is crowded, so prefer a twist (anagram ladders, hidden-word grid).
6. **Number/estimation and Gardner-style text puzzles (Math slot).** Weighings, river crossings, cutting/folding counts, "how many handshakes." Why: short text answer, verified by brute force, aha-rich. Effort S. Static.
7. **Mini-meta in the weekly deep slot.** 4-5 small clue puzzles whose answers spell a final word (Mystery Hunt shape [20]). Why: strong aha and "I figured it out" payoff; fits "deep problem." Effort M (content craft). Static; owner approval per set.
8. **Connections-style grouping (words only, human-reviewed).** Why: second most played NYT game [1], and LLM drafts are near NYT-level [5]. Concern: correctness is judgment-based, so the answer key risk is real; conflicts with "every key must be correct" unless groups are hard-verified (e.g. hidden-word/anagram/rhyme categories checkable by script). Effort M. Static. Recommend "script-verifiable categories only."
9. **Sudoku/kakuro/nonogram-style grid puzzles.** Why: canonical, unique, easy to verify [13]. Cost: sudoku is saturated; nonograms need a big grid UI and take 10+ min. Effort L. Place after #3-#4.
10. **Chess mate-in-2 puzzle (optional weekly).** Why: verified by engine, 1-3 minutes; ties to owner's history. Effort M (board UI, FEN, legal-move check). Requires chess literacy, so keep as a bonus, not core.

Skip/flag: cryptic crosswords (answer checkable, quality not; low volume), rebus/lateral thinking as core (ambiguous answers), geography/film/music Wordle-likes (need lookup, short-lived [17]), escape rooms, and anything with speed leaderboards or share-grids that create streak pressure. A text-only share result (e.g. "Way 11/05: 4/5 solved") is consistent with the no-scroll principle, but it is the sharing hook that grew Wordle [3], so flag it as a deliberate choice. Do NOT add infinite mode: Gage's own caps (5 hands/day, 10 hours of fun) support Way's stop-line [7][9].

Interactive-grid effort summary
- Text/short-answer only (no grid): knights-knaves, zebra (typed), Gardner/math, mini-meta, word hook. Build these first.
- Grid UI needed: Queens, Tango, Zip, Sudoku, nonograms, chess board. One shared tap-grid component should serve the first three.

Generation pipeline suggestion (inference): for each type, write (a) generator or AI draft, (b) solver that counts solutions and rates difficulty, (c) JSON export. Reject any item with solution count != 1. This matches the owner's existing "AI drafts, scripts verify, owner approves" workflow.
