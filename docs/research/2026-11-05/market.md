# Way: Market and competitor research (date: 2026-11-05)

Method note: WebSearch plus WebFetch. Search quality was uneven. Many results were aggregators, affiliate sites or AI-generated pages; I flag these. Where I could not find a primary source I say "not found". I did not find usable Hacker News or Reddit threads on Brilliant, Duolingo Math/Chess, Imprint, Peak or Synthesis, so complaint patterns for those rest on review aggregators and are weaker evidence.

## 1. Key findings

- **Billing and paywall friction is the dominant complaint in paid learning apps.** Brilliant (trial-to-annual surprise charges, refund refusals, thin content for price) [1][2]; Headway (billing, cancellation, fake urgency timers; per a rival-adjacent blog, so biased) [3]; Blinkist (pricing, subscription process) [3]. Lumosity, Elevate and Peak cap the free tier at about 3 games a day (one critical ranking; vendor blog) [4].
- **Brain-training "transfer" is a discredited promise.** FTC fined Lumosity $2M in January 2016 for lacking evidence for real-world benefit claims; a 2016 review found little evidence of transfer beyond the trained tasks [5]. Elevate's only direct evidence was a company-commissioned, non-peer-reviewed study [5]. Way should not claim to make people smarter.
- **Daily-puzzle products win on a finite, shared, once-a-day ritual.** Wordle's creator said he distrusts apps that demand attention and send push notifications, and wanted a few minutes a day, not more [6]. Puzzmo's Zach Gage caps play (five Pile-Up Poker hands a day, would prefer three) so players never ask "what am I doing?" [7].
- **The category is big and monetised by subscription.** NYT Games puzzles were played 11.1 billion times in the prior year per NYT figures relayed to The Verge in June 2025 (second-hand via Wikipedia mirror) [8]. NYT put the Mini Crossword behind a paywall in late August 2025 and Reddit threads asked for free alternatives [9]. Puzzmo: free games plus roughly $40/year paid tier as of 2023 [7b].
- **Free and ad-free is rare and trusted but usually unsustainable except as non-profit.** Lichess is donation-funded, ad-free, "always free" (French non-profit) [10]. Puzzle-app users complain about interstitial ads between short puzzles and hint paywalls [11].
- **The "anti-AI-offloading" positioning already has one commercial entrant.** Spark by The Mind Company (formerly Elevate Labs), launched 12 Nov 2025: 3 daily handcrafted trivia-style puzzles, free daily set, $11.99/yr archive, explicitly positioned against "cognitive offloading" to AI [12]. This is the closest competitor found. It is knowledge/trivia (history, science, pop culture), not reasoning across Words/Logic/Math/Code/Create, and it has an account/subscription model.
- **Cultural tailwind is real but partly overhyped.** "Brain rot" was Oxford's 2024 Word of the Year (about 37,000 public votes, 230% usage rise) [13]. MIT Media Lab's "Your Brain on ChatGPT" preprint (June 2025; 54 participants, EEG, not peer reviewed, 18 in the crossover session) fuels "cognitive debt" talk [14]. Dumbphone demand is survey-backed (Morning Consult: 16% of Gen Z adults own one; 64% of Gen Z spend 5+ hours a day on phones) but sales growth claims are unverified [15].
- **Backlash rarely hurts incumbents financially.** Duolingo's April 2025 "AI-first" memo drew heavy social backlash, yet Q2 2025 DAUs grew 40% YoY and revenue beat estimates [16]. Inference: anti-AI sentiment is real among a vocal slice, but it does not by itself move mass-market retention, so Way should treat it as a niche identity, not a mass-market growth thesis.

### 2.2 Puzzle products

- **NYT Games.** Subscription (Games sub plus some free dailies). Wordle acquisition (Jan 2022) added "tens of millions" of users per NYT [20]. 2024: over 10 million daily players and over one million premium subscribers (Wikipedia-derived; low confidence) [8]. Praise (inference from reporting): trusted human editing, shareable result grid. Complaint: creeping paywalls, e.g. Mini Crossword in Aug 2025 [9].
- **Puzzmo.** Hearst-owned. Mostly free, about $40/yr to unlock archive, leaderboards and bonus games (2023 AMA; may have changed) [7b]. Design philosophy: capped play, algorithmic generation where possible, rejects dark patterns [7]. This is the closest in spirit to Way's restraint, but it is a game portal, not a "thinking" product, and it is subscription funded.
- **LinkedIn Games.** Free, launched 1 May 2024, one play per day per game, friend scores. Motivation: engagement for the host platform [21]. No usage figures found.
- **Chess.com vs Lichess puzzles.** Lichess: donation-funded, no ads, all features free [10]. Chess.com: ads in free tier; unlimited puzzles behind premium; user complaints about a few puzzles per day then upsell (anecdotal; current limit not verified) [10].
- **Advent of Code.** Free, once a year (December). 2024: 284,977 solvers of day 1, 23,170,305 total stars. For 2025 the creator cut it to 12 days and removed the global leaderboard (announced 22 Oct 2025) [22]. Shows demand for finite, seasonal, crafted problems and for deliberate de-escalation.
- **Project Euler, LeetCode, Codewars, Exercism, Sporcle, Puzzling Stack Exchange.** Not covered by usable search results; see "not found" below. (Inference only: these serve a single audience: programmers, trivia fans, or puzzle hobbyists.)
- **Small free ad-free daily sites.** A Show HN ("Got sick of ads, so I made my own logic puzzle site", 251 points, 167 comments, date shown only as "4 months ago") drew these complaints: forced sign-up (most common), unclear pricing, suspected LLM-generated puzzles and design, puzzles with multiple solutions, error-flagging that "gives away answers", requests for offline PWA and non-competitive mode [23]. PuzzleBoxs and Patternle are other free/no-ads daily sites; neither mentions AI [12-search].

## 3. Trends 2024-2026

- **Brain rot and screen anxiety:** Oxford 2024 [13]; Gen Z phone-time data [15].
- **AI dependence:** MIT preprint, widely covered, caveated by small sample [14]. Spark launched on this theme [12]. Inference: "anti-AI-dependency" is entering product marketing in late 2025, so a first-mover claim is gone, but a credible and honest version is still open.
- **Daily puzzle growth:** NYT play counts [8]; LinkedIn and others adding games to retain users [21]. Inference: big platforms use puzzles to harvest attention. Way's contrast is that it does not.
- **Subscription fatigue:** Forrester: about half of US online adults cancelled a subscription in 12 months, two-thirds wish they could pause [24]. Deloitte 2025 (streaming, via secondary reports): 41% say content is not worth the price [24].
- **Ads in puzzle apps:** Interstitials between short puzzles and hint/error mechanics steering toward ads or upgrades are recurring complaints (vendor blog and one Reddit mirror; anecdotal) [11].
- **Dumbphones:** Real but small. Light Phone says 100,000+ users; sales growth figures are unverified [15].

## 4. The gap and the nearest competitors

Way's combination: free, no ads, no account, finite 5-problem set, one per area (Words, Logic, Math, Code, Create), hints that are questions, no answers before commitment, no streaks, explicit stance on AI offloading.

| Needs | Nearest | How Way differs |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-AI-offloading daily puzzle | **Spark (Mind Company)** | Trivia/knowledge vs effortful reasoning; free vs $11.99 archive; apps with accounts vs static local-first; Way has Code and Create and a coach-style hint ladder. |
| Restraint and anti-dark-pattern design | **Puzzmo, Wordle** | Puzzmo is games with subscription; Way is thinking across subjects. Wordle is one word game. |
| Free, no ads, trusted | **Lichess, KrazyDad, small indie sites** | Single domain each. Way adds variety. |
| Reasoning variety | **Brilliant** | Brilliant is a paid curriculum, big content volume, no daily boundary. Way is free and finite. |
| Daily shared set | **NYT, LinkedIn** | Platform/ad/subscription incentives. |

Not found: any product that is free, ad-free, account-free, cross-subject (including code and open creativity), has question-form hints, and ends after a finite set. Not found does not prove it does not exist.

## 5. Implications for Way (ranked)

1. **Position as "a daily workout you finish", not a brain-training or learning app.** What: lead copy with finite, 20 minutes, done. Why: FTC/transfer history means health or IQ claims are a liability [5]; Wordle/Puzzmo show restraint is the draw [6][7]. Fit: strong (no infinite mode). Effort: S. Cost: none.
2. **Never promise cognitive benefit; frame as "practice thinking" and be explicit on AI.** Why: Spark already claims "measurably more knowledgeable" without data [12]; MIT study is a preprint with 54 people [14]. Honest, modest framing is a differentiator. Fit: strong. Effort: S.
3. **Turn the hint-ladder-as-questions into the signature feature and market it.** Why: the Show HN thread shows users hate hints/error flags that give answers away [23]; sudoku apps push hints toward ads [11]. Fit: core principle. Effort: S (copy and demo), M if adding more hint tuning. Cost: none.
4. **Keep "no account, no sign-up" as a headline.** Why: forced sign-up was the top complaint on a small ad-free puzzle site [23]; Brilliant/Headway billing anger [1][3]. Fit: strong. Effort: S. Cost: none.
5. **Copy the shared-date ritual with a shareable result (no numbers about people).** Why: Wordle family-chat sharing [6]; NYT results grid (inference). Add an emoji-grid share of the area map or a per-problem result, with no tracking. Fit: good if share is opt-in. Effort: S-M. Cost: none.
6. **Differentiate from Spark on subject: lean into reasoning and making (Logic, Math, Code, Create).** Why: Spark is trivia/knowledge [12]; Lumosity-style apps are interchangeable mini-games [4]. Fit: strong. Effort: M (content). Cost: none.
7. **Offer a PWA / offline mode and a non-competitive, mistake-tolerant check button.** Why: both were top requests in the Show HN thread [23]. Fit: strong. Effort: M. Cost: none (static).
8. **Quality bar on answer keys and AI-origin honesty.** Why: users in the Show HN thread suspect "LLM-generated" puzzles and found multi-solution puzzles [23]; Way's AI-draft plus script-verify plus human approval pipeline is a trust story. Consider a visible "verified by script, approved by a human" note. Fit: strong. Effort: S.
9. **Keep it free; plan sustainability now (donations, sponsorships from non-ad sources, or open-source content).** Why: Lichess shows free at scale needs a non-profit/donation model [10]; Puzzmo and Spark both ended up charging for archive/extras [7b][12]. Do not add a paywall (anti-goal); decide before traffic. Effort: S (decision), M (donation link). Runtime cost: $0 now; a donation link needs no server.
10. **Avoid copying from Duolingo and platform games: streaks, leagues, notifications.** Why: Duolingo succeeds financially despite backlash [16], so do not infer streaks are universally hated; they conflict with Way's anti-goals regardless. The Done-screen area map is a good alternative; keep it.
11. **Do not try to match Brilliant's content volume.** Why: Brilliant's strength is a paid curriculum; its complaint is thin value for the price [1]. Way's finite daily set with an archive possibility later is a different promise. Revisit an archive only if it stays non-addictive (it conflicts with "no archive" currently).
12. **Learn from Advent of Code's seasonal, deliberately de-escalated shape.** Why: it cut to 12 days and dropped the global leaderboard to reduce pressure [22]. Possible "season" format (e.g. a December set) without streaks. Effort: M. Cost: none.

## 6. Risks

- **Sustainability:** free with no ads, no accounts, no analytics means no revenue and no usage data; it relies on $0 hosting and owner time. Content volume is human-approved, so scaling problems is the bottleneck, not servers.
- **Content volume vs Brilliant and NYT:** a 5-per-day set is 1,825 problems per year; keeping quality high while verifying answers is costly.
- **No analytics** means Way cannot measure whether it replaces doomscrolling. Consider an optional, local-only self-report.
- **Spark/Elevate has brand, funding and app-store presence** and may expand beyond trivia.
- **Anti-AI stance risk:** vocal but not necessarily mass; also Way uses AI to draft problems; be transparent.
- **Evidence risk:** many of my numbers (NYT games subscribers, dumbphone growth, Brilliant/Lumosity prices) are second-hand or conflicting; re-verify before quoting publicly.
