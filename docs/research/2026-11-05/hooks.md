# Engagement hooks and habit design without guilt (research track: hooks)

Date of research: 2026-11-05. FACT = sourced. INFERENCE/OPINION = mine. "Not found" means not found in the searches run.

## 1. Key findings

- **Wordle's design rules were deliberate.** Wardle said one puzzle per day creates scarcity, credited NYT Spelling Bee as inspiration, and said an earlier prototype allowed endless play [1][2]. He said "no ads, not doing anything with your data" is deliberate, and he distrusts apps that want endless attention [2]. TIME: the game had no ads or push notifications and he said monetizing "does not really appeal" to him [3].
- **Sharing drove growth, but the grid was a user invention.** Players in New Zealand hand-typed colored squares on Twitter; Wardle then added an automatic share button (reported as 16 Dec 2021) [4][5]. Growth: about 90 players on 1 Nov 2021 to over 300,000 by 2 Jan 2022 (secondary sources; the 300k is variously "daily" or "monthly") [5]. The Spinoff says under 100 players to over 2 million in under three months [4]. I found no primary confirmation of these numbers.
- **The grid works because it is spoiler-free and tells a story without revealing the answer.** Secondary sources only [5]. (INFERENCE: this is the single most transferable mechanic for Way.)
- **Puzzmo (Zach Gage) openly designs against infinite play.** Pile-Up Poker is capped at 5 hands a day; Gage would personally prefer 3. He does not want players to ask "what am I doing right now?", says constraint makes a game more interesting, and names "dark patterns of apps built to offer infinite play" as something to resist (Aug 2024) [6]. Puzzmo uses slow growth (about 500 new players a day), and gates groups, leaderboards and archives behind a subscription (Nov 2023) [7]. Neither article discusses streaks, so Puzzmo's streak stance is **not found**.
- **NYT Games layers stats and analysis on top of one daily puzzle.** Stats pages show completion, win rate, streaks, no-hint solves [8]. WordleBot (Katz and Conlen, 2022; subscriber feature) scores each guess for skill and luck against an information-theory solver [9]. NYT counts 11.1 billion puzzles solved in 2024 [8] (volume, not unique players). A YourDictionary survey reports roughly 12 minutes/day for Wordle players; sample method unclear [10].
- **Duolingo's own published numbers are mostly correlational and tiny.** Blog (31 Jan 2022): 6M+ learners had 7+ day streaks; reaching 7 days makes learners 3.6x more likely to finish a course (the post does not say causal); a milestone animation raised new-learner 7-day return by 1.7%; allowing two equipped Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by 0.38% (relative) [11]. The same post cites research that some slack motivates more than rigid rules [11]. A qualitative study reports apprehension and anxiety from gamification misuse in a language app [12]. I found no rigorous study measuring streak guilt at scale; the rest is opinion pieces.
- **Friction works, and a "dismiss" option works best.** The one sec PNAS paper (Feb 2023; 280 participants, 6 weeks) reports a 57% decrease in actual opening of target apps after six weeks; 36% of attempts ended with the app closed; attempts fell 37% from week 1. A dismiss option had the strongest effect; the time delay also helped; the "deliberation message" was not effective [13]. The 57% on one sec's own pages is sometimes phrased differently, so cite the abstract. CHI 2024 (1,039 one sec users): frictions reduce attempts and make opens more intentional, but users rebound quickly after breaks [14][15].
- **Intercepting another app's launch is limited on iOS.** one sec works via a Shortcuts personal automation "When <app> is opened, run ..." [16]. No app can observe another app's launch directly, so the automation is the only hook [17]. Redirecting to a different app than the one opened is not documented by Apple; an Apple DTS reply (2024, via a developer forum thread) found no supported way to send the user back on to the opened app [16]. Looping when re-opening the same app is a known pitfall [17]. For a redirect to a different destination there is no loop, but I did not find a source confirming it works end to end. **Needs a hands-on test.**
- **Habit science supports tiny, cued, forgiving routines.** Fogg: behavior = Motivation x Ability x Prompt; raising ability (making it easier) is usually more dependable than raising motivation; recipe "After I [anchor], I will [tiny behavior], then celebrate" [18]. Gollwitzer and Sheeran (2006): if-then plans have a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.65, 94 tests) [19]. Lally et al.: median 66 days to automaticity (range 18 to 254 modeled), and one missed day did not significantly hurt habit formation, though very inconsistent people did not form habits [20].
- **Eyal's Hook model is disputed.** Trigger, action, variable reward, investment; critics call it a compulsion loop with a weak evidence base [21]. *Indistractable* was not covered by my sources, so I do not cite it. INFERENCE: borrow "internal trigger" (a feeling that cues the app, e.g. restless boredom at bedtime) but avoid variable reward and investment-as-lock-in.
- **Calm technology** (Amber Case): needs the smallest possible amount of attention; moves easily between periphery and center; communicates without speaking [22]. Center for Humane Technology design guide: **not found** (fetch returned 404), so no claims made.
- **Project Euler gates its per-problem forum behind solving the problem.** Community discussion unlocks after you solve it [23] (forum posts, older; not an official statement).

## 2. Detail

### 2.1 What Wordle's retention actually rests on
FACTS: one puzzle a day, same for everyone, no ads/notifications/data use, spoiler-free share. INFERENCE: the daily limit makes the puzzle a shared event and gives a natural end ("come back tomorrow"). The share grid turns a private result into a social cue without needing accounts, a server or a leaderboard. Retention came from the ritual and conversation, not from engineered pressure. Wardle sold to NYT in early 2022 for "low seven figures" [3].

### 2.2 Stats, bots and percentiles without guilt
NYT and Puzzmo both put analysis after the puzzle. WordleBot is the clearest model: it compares your path with an optimal one and rates skill and luck [9]. INFERENCE: this suits Way's coach stance. An "after-action review" is natural for Logic, Math, Code (e.g., "your solution passes; here is a shorter way"). Percentile comparisons need a server and aggregate data; Way has none. A local-only variant is possible: compare against your own past hint usage ("fewer hints than your last 10 Logic problems"). That is private, offline, and never says you failed.

### 2.3 Streaks, freezes and gentler alternatives
Duolingo's published evidence shows streaks and freezes help retention at the margin (0.38% to 1.7% relative lifts) [11]; the large "3.6x" figure is correlation. Critics and a qualitative study link the mechanic to anxiety [12]. Duolingo itself added freezes and cites slack as more motivating than rigid rules [11], an admission that pure streaks are brittle. Gentler options (INFERENCE, my designs, not sourced evidence): a month calendar of "visited" days with no counter; weekly "touched N of 5 areas"; a mastery map by area; "welcome back" with no mention of absence. Way's area map already fits this family. Lally's finding that one missed day barely matters [20] supports not punishing gaps.

### 2.4 Intercepting social apps
one sec is the best-evidenced pattern in this space: put a pause where the habit fires [13]. Dismiss ("I don't need this") beat the delay and beat reflection prompts [13], so a redirect should be a choice, not a lecture. Rebound after breaks [14] means this is a standing routine, not a cure. Related tools: ScreenZen (free pause plus open limits; needs Accessibility on Android; vendor-comparison sources only), Opal (schedule-based blocks, Screen Time API), Brick (physical puck, one-time hardware price) [24]. Android Digital Wellbeing: **not found** in my searches.

**Way as the redirect target (INFERENCE, needs testing):** iOS Shortcuts personal automation "When Instagram/TikTok/YouTube is opened" -> action "Open URL" `https://<way-domain>/` with "Run Immediately". Apple documents Open URL for launching URLs [25]. Unverified points: (a) that a personal automation can open a URL without a confirmation tap; (b) whether the URL opens in Safari or the installed home-screen app (my expectation is Safari, because links open in the browser, unconfirmed); (c) whether the opened social app is left behind in the background. Way should ship a short "set this up" page with a Shortcuts recipe and a screenshot, and test on a real device before promising anything. Shortcuts also lets a shortcut be placed on the Home Screen as an icon, which one sec suggests to avoid the "glimpse" of the target app [17].

### 2.5 PWA install, widgets
FACTS (secondary): iOS 16.4 added Web Push and the Badging API for Home Screen web apps only; Safari data can be evicted after about a week of disuse and an installed app is reportedly exempt, but Apple forum posters say Apple does not document this; Safari storage and the Home Screen app storage are separate [26][27]. INFERENCE: Way is local-first, so progress would live in localStorage/IndexedDB. A user who plays in Safari and later installs will appear to lose history, and non-installed users risk eviction. Offer export/import (a text code) and a plain explanation. Push is irrelevant because Way has no notifications.

### 2.6 Social without leaderboards
Options by cost: share card (client-only); "send this problem to a friend" via a URL containing the problem id (static, no server); friend duel (both solve the same daily problem and compare a result string by chat; no server); post-solve discussion (Project Euler pattern [23]) needs moderation and a server. Puzzmo keeps groups/leaderboards as paid, small-scale features [7]. INFERENCE: leaderboards invite comparison pressure and conflict with Way's anti-goals, so skip them.

### 2.7 Session design
Gage: constrain play; fewer than you think (3 to 5 hands) [6]. Wordle players report about 12 minutes/day (low-quality survey) [10]. Way's set is about 20 minutes, which is long next to puzzle-app norms. INFERENCE: keep the full set, but make a "short mode" (first 2 problems then Done, nothing lost) so bedtime sessions can shrink. Not found: a rigorous study of ideal daily session length for puzzle apps.

## 3. Implications for Way (ranked)

1. **Spoiler-free share card for the Done screen.** What: copy-to-clipboard text such as "Way 2026-11-05 / Words 🟩 Logic 🟨 Math 🟩 Code ⬜ Create ✍" (colors = no hints / hints / gave up; Create = a marker), plus the day number and URL. Why: Wordle's growth came from exactly this [4][5]; spoiler-free results give conversation without answers. Fit: no scroll, no guilt, no answers revealed; do not show "failure" in red, use neutral symbols. Effort: S. Server: no.
2. **Intercept-the-feed recipe (Shortcuts "App is opened" to Way).** What: a /setup page with a copy-paste recipe for iOS Shortcuts (and an Android launcher/ScreenZen note), a deep link that opens today's Words problem directly. Why: friction at the moment of the habit works [13]; this is the core of Way's mission. Fit: fully voluntary, user-installed, no nagging; offer an easy off switch; make the pause a choice (dismiss) not a lecture [13]. Risks: iOS behavior unverified (see 2.4); rebound after breaks [14]. Effort: S to build page, M to test devices. Server: no.
3. **Installable PWA with offline play and export/import.** What: manifest, service worker, "Add to Home Screen" explainer, an export code for progress. Why: a home-screen icon is the stable prompt and can sit where Instagram was; storage caveats [26][27]. Fit: no push, no badge; do not use the Badging API (a badge is a red-dot mechanic). Effort: M. Server: no.
4. **Gentle progress: keep the area map, add a private "months" calendar and a mastery level per area.** What: tiles that brighten with practice and fade slowly; a calendar of days played with no counters; per-area "Seen / Steady / Fluent" from hints used over time. Why: Duolingo's own data show streak lifts are small and slack motivates [11]; one missed day barely matters for habits [20]. Fit: no streak, no red, no loss language; fading must be soft and never say "lost". Effort: M. Server: no (localStorage).
5. **Post-solve "how you did" review (a WordleBot-like coach, local).** What: after each problem, compare your hints and time with your own history, and show one better path. Why: NYT's most loved add-on is analysis [9]; fits "coach, not oracle". Fit: only after commitment; no leaderboard. Effort: M. Server: no. (Global percentiles would need a server; defer.)
6. **"Send this problem to a friend" link and friend duel string.** What: `/p/<id>` link shows the problem without the answer; both players post their result string. Why: the Wordle social loop [4]; Project Euler gates discussion on solving [23]. Fit: invitation only, no feed, no ranks. Effort: S to M. Server: no (static id routes), but a static site must ship all problems or pre-generated pages. Risk: link-sharing reveals future daily problems if ids are guessable; use only past or today's problems.
7. **Short mode / "Just the first two".** What: an explicit, guilt-free short path for tired nights, ending in the normal Done screen. Why: Gage's cap philosophy [6]; Fogg: ability beats motivation [18]. Fit: makes ending easier, supports anti-scroll. Effort: S. Server: no.
8. **If-then cue copy at first run.** What: one optional line on first visit: "After I put my phone on the charger, I do one problem" (a Gollwitzer if-then, Fogg's anchor) and a calendar-file reminder the user creates themselves, not a notification. Why: if-then plans d = 0.65 [19]; Fogg recipe [18]. Fit: self-chosen, no nagging; keep it skippable. Effort: S. Server: no.
9. **Post-solve discussion (Project Euler pattern).** What: comments on a solved problem. Why: deepens engagement [23]. Fit: conflicts with "no accounts, no server"; also becomes a feed if unbounded. Effort: L. Server: yes. Recommend deferring; maybe link to a GitHub Discussions thread per problem as a $0 stand-in.
10. **Percentile and leaderboard features.** Not recommended: they need a server and aggregate data, and invite comparison pressure. Flagged as conflicting with anti-goals.

Not recommended without flagging a conflict: streak counters with freezes (guilt mechanic [11][12]), push notifications and badges (Way's explicit anti-goal; Wardle also avoided them [3]), variable-reward mechanics from the Hook model (compulsion loop critique [21]), "hint economy" currencies (Not researched in depth; I found no primary source on Puzzmo or NYT hint economies).
